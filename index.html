About Me

Welcome.

I've never fit neatly into one box. Mixed race, I wasn't "black enough" or "white enough" depending on the room. I had no interest in sports — I'd rather be alone in my room, putting on fashion shows for my stuffed animals. For years it felt like standing outside every category.

Looking back, I can see what that does to a person. It's exhausting to keep explaining yourself, adjusting yourself, or wondering whether something about you is just wrong. If you've done that your whole life, you already know the feeling I mean.

Standing outside made me an observer. I became curious about why people think the way they do, how behavior develops, and how much our surroundings shape us. I thought healthcare was where I belonged, but biology never clicked the way psychology did — so I followed the curiosity instead.

Early in my career I worked in inpatient behavioral health. I watched the same patients get discharged and come back two weeks later, no better than when they left. And I saw who got labeled what — Black kids called "aggressive" or "bipolar" while white kids with the same behavior got "ADHD" or "impulsive." It echoed something I already knew first-hand: difference treated as a defect.

Working in autism services changed how I thought about the work. Instead of asking how to make someone look more typical, I started asking how to help them build a life that fits who they are. A lot of what gets called a social deficit is really just a difference. So I stopped treating quirks and intense interests as problems to fix. I help people understand how their own minds work and build around that — so they can live true to themselves while still navigating the world.

That's also why I've never been drawn to rigid, by-the-book scripts — they rarely fit the person in front of me any better than society's boxes fit me. My approach is insight-driven: before we reach for techniques, I want to understand what's happening underneath. Once we understand what's driving it, we decide together which tools actually fit you.

Today I run a fully virtual practice for adults who've felt misunderstood for most of their lives — people with ADHD, OCD, autism, anxiety, a late diagnosis, an LGBTQ+ identity, or just the sense that they've never quite fit. You won't have to translate yourself here. I don't believe your identity is the problem to solve.

The Process

How we
work together

Every step is collaborative, paced by you, and grounded in evidence-based methods adapted for neurodivergent minds. You're never locked into a rigid protocol.

Modalities: CBT · ERP · Integrative · Strengths-Based · Relational

01
Follow What Is Present
Sessions begin with what is most present for you — what is on your mind, what is pressing, what connects to why you came to therapy. No agenda imposed from outside.
02
Surface the Pattern
From what emerges, we extract the deeper details and map them to the patterns that connect to your goals. Insight comes before action — always.
03
Apply Structure Selectively
ERP for OCD. CBT for thought patterns. Workbooks when structure supports the insight. Methods are tools, not templates — applied only where they genuinely help.
04
Review at 90 Days
Goals are reviewed every 90 days. Progress is assessed, direction is adjusted, and the work stays connected to what you actually came in for.
